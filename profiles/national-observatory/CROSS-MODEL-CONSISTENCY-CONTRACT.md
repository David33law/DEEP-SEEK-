# National Legal Observatory — Cross-Model Consistency Contract v1

## Purpose

Passing independent arenas is necessary but not sufficient. A candidate could expose one legal-time model in `candidate.py`, another in its bounded transition models and a third in its public interoperability projections. Such a system would contain several incompatible conceptions of the law while each local test still appeared correct.

Every surviving architecture must therefore pass a hidden cross-model witness campaign over the same legal history.

## Artifacts under comparison

The evaluator receives only already-built candidate sources:

- the selected semantic `candidate.py`;
- both qualified `formal_candidate.py` implementations;
- both qualified `interoperability_candidate.py` implementations.

Builders do not receive the witness history, expected answers or evaluator source.

## Shared legal history

The hidden generator creates randomized stable identities, evidence identifiers, texts, dates and ECLI values and exercises:

- original legislation;
- amendment;
- a later-known correction with an earlier legal effective time;
- repeal and revival;
- duplicate evidence;
- conflicting equal-authority evidence;
- doctrine attachment;
- jurisprudence linking at an historical legal/knowledge cut;
- provenance, replay and multi-channel publication.

The semantic and formal interfaces receive equivalent typed events, adapted only to their declared interface vocabulary.

## Required agreement

The evaluator compares normalized legal observations, not implementation-specific state-root algorithms.

Both semantic and formal models must agree on:

- active/in-force, repealed and unknown status;
- provision text at each legal-time and knowledge-time cut;
- the evidence set supporting each state;
- the fact that a later-known correction changes historical reconstruction only after its knowledge time;
- repeal/revival behavior;
- duplicate idempotence;
- conflict observability;
- crash/partition state not inventing legal authority where the formal model exposes those actions.

The semantic implementation must additionally demonstrate:

- doctrine does not alter binding state;
- jurisprudence links to the exact version evidence applicable at the decision cut;
- provenance contains the evidence needed for the published state;
- replay is deterministic;
- every publication channel is a projection of the same state.

Both interoperability implementations must project the exact normalized semantic state, stable work/expression/manifestation/provision-version identities, normative impacts, ECLI link, evidence hashes, unknowns and canonical publication root.

## Root boundary

Candidate-defined semantic replay roots, formal model roots, durable roots and projection roots need not use the same byte algorithm unless the blueprint explicitly says they do. The campaign therefore compares legal observations and verifies one-root consistency within each artifact. It must never claim equality of unrelated root algorithms.

## Independence

Qualification, independent replication and crown use fresh hidden histories. No revision is allowed during cross-model replication or crown. A qualification failure may use one aggregate-diagnostic architecture-preserving revision only through the ordinary semantic/formal/interoperability revision paths; the cross-model evaluator itself never rewrites candidate code.

## Terminal consequence

`cross_model_consistency_passed`, `cross_model_replication_passed` and `cross_model_crown_passed` are explicit supremacy conditions.

A legal-state disagreement, missing qualified artifact, conflict hidden by one model, incompatible public projection or unsupported root-equivalence claim blocks `COMMITTED`. Resource exhaustion yields only `BEST_DISCOVERED_SO_FAR`.

# National Legal Observatory — Authoritative Prior-Art Challenge Contract v1

## 1. Purpose

Independent model search is not sufficient evidence that the attainable architecture space was covered. Models may share training-induced attractors, omit known standards, underweight formal-method families or reinvent weaker versions of established public mechanisms.

After an executable CP2-blind frontier exists, the production protocol must challenge that frontier against the owner-signed `PUBLIC-PRIOR-ART-MANIFEST.json`.

The manifest is a falsification corpus, not a design template. No listed standard, paper, implementation or institution receives architectural authority merely by being listed.

## 2. Timing and blindness

The prior-art corpus must remain closed during:

- initial independent and anti-attractor search;
- selected-seed expansion;
- v0 formalization and prebuild destruction;
- initial candidate construction;
- first hidden semantic and durable qualification;
- creation of the first independent executable Pareto frontier.

It may be opened only by the dedicated prior-art challenge overlay after that frontier exists. Earlier CP2 research remains a separate quarantined source and is not merged into this manifest.

## 3. Source discipline

Every source entry must identify:

- a stable `source_id`;
- the responsible standards body, institution or primary research publisher;
- status and version as of the owner-signed manifest date;
- an authoritative public locator;
- concise load-bearing claims derived from the source;
- controlled architecture axes that the source can challenge.

The manifest freezes the research obligation, not the remote website bytes. The run may not claim that the external source endorses the Observatory or that the source alone proves an architecture.

## 4. Independent review portfolio

Every prior-art wave must run three independent critics with non-overlapping primary responsibilities:

1. **legal interoperability critic** — legal identity, ELI/ECLI, Akoma Ntoso, LegalRuleML, temporal legal effect and multi-channel publication;
2. **provenance/governance critic** — PROV, SHACL, transparency, trust succession, split-view resistance and independently verifiable publication;
3. **formal/distributed systems critic** — state-machine refinement, crash correctness, concurrency, replication, partition assumptions, recovery and proof boundaries.

Each critic must assess every source ID, even when it finds the source inapplicable to its specialty. Missing source coverage blocks the wave.

## 5. Review outcomes

For every source, each critic returns one of:

- **SATISFIED** — the measured frontier already contains executable evidence that addresses the source-derived challenge;
- **PARTIAL** — part of the challenge is addressed but a constructible architecture or proof obligation remains;
- **MISSING** — the frontier omits a constructible load-bearing mechanism or architecture family;
- **INAPPLICABLE** — the source claim does not bear on the Observatory mission or the current architecture, with a falsifiable reason.

A `SATISFIED` disposition must cite persisted frontier, semantic, durable, proof or governance evidence. A narrative assertion is not evidence.

## 6. Constructible challengers

Every PARTIAL or MISSING architectural gap must produce a complete controlled-genome proposal or be marked as a protocol/evaluator blocker.

A proposed prior-art challenger must include:

- all source IDs that motivate it;
- a complete architecture blueprint and controlled genome;
- the exact frontier assumption it attacks;
- the expected falsifiable improvement;
- the cost or stronger assumption it introduces;
- why it is not simply a standard-shaped renaming of an existing genome.

All distinct challenger genomes are expanded, formalized, pre-attacked and constructed through the same semantic and durable paths as every other candidate. They receive no favorable scoring for matching a known standard.

## 7. Protocol and evaluator gaps

Some source-derived gaps cannot be discharged by a new candidate because the current evaluator cannot observe the claimed property. Examples include an untested distributed consistency model, missing standards conformance validation or absence of a machine-checkable proof boundary.

Such a gap is a protocol blocker. It requires a new owner-signed harness revision and cannot be converted into `SATISFIED`, a dry novelty wave or a supremacy claim by model judgment.

## 8. Consensus and fail-closed behavior

All source IDs must be assessed by all three critics.

If any critic reports PARTIAL or MISSING:

- every supplied distinct challenger is retained and constructed;
- absence of a challenger requires an explicit blocker;
- conflicting assessments remain evidence and are included in later destroyer/lower-bound context.

A campaign closes only when:

- every manifest source was assessed by all critics;
- every constructible challenger was built and entered the ordinary frontier review;
- every protocol/evaluator blocker is closed by a new owner-signed revision;
- no source review remains unresolved;
- the campaign artifact is persisted and bound to the manifest hash.

## 9. Terminal consequence

`prior_art_challenged` is an explicit supremacy condition in the signed terminal schema.

COMMITTED is forbidden unless the final incumbent has faced all manifest-derived constructible challengers and no prior-art blocker remains. Resource exhaustion can produce only `BEST_DISCOVERED_SO_FAR`.

## 10. Public claim discipline

The final supremacy dossier must identify which public standards and primary research patterns were used as challenges, what architectural alternatives they generated, and what executable or formal evidence defeated or incorporated them.

It must not imply endorsement by the standards bodies, authors, institutions, Elon Musk, xAI, SpaceX, DeepMind, DARPA or any other third party.
